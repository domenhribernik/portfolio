Privacy and cookies

This is a personal site. Most of it stores nothing about you at all: the games, the maps and the toys run entirely in your browser. Where something is stored, this page says what, why, for how long, and how to get rid of it.

Who is responsible

This site is run by Domen Hribernik as a private individual in Slovenia, not as a registered business. For the purposes of the GDPR I am the data controller. There is no data protection officer, because a site this size is nowhere near needing one.

Written questions, requests and complaints all go to contact@domenhribernik.com.

What is collected, and why

One entry per thing the site actually does. If a feature is not listed here, it does not send anything to the server.

An account, if you make one

Signing in is optional and only needed for the tools that save your own work. Sign-in goes through Google, which tells me your Google account identifier, your email address, your display name and the URL of your profile picture. I store those so the account exists and so your saved work comes back to you.

Legal basis: performance of a contract, in the sense that you asked for an account and this is what an account is made of. Kept: until you delete the account, which you can do yourself from the account page.

Sign-in sessions and failed sign-ins

While you are signed in, the server keeps a session row holding your IP address and browser user agent. Failed sign-in attempts are recorded the same way. This is how the site tells your session apart from someone else's, and how it slows down anyone trying passwords in bulk.

Legal basis: legitimate interest in keeping accounts from being broken into. Kept: 30 days, then deleted automatically.

The contact form

Sending me a message stores the name, email address and message you typed. It also stores your browser user agent and a one way hash of your IP address, salted and rotated daily, which lets me spot a flood of spam without keeping anyone's actual IP address. The message is pushed to me over Telegram so I see it, and it stays in the site's database so I can find it again.

Legal basis: legitimate interest in answering people who write to me. Kept: 12 months.

The Everbloom waitlist and the quote form

The Everbloom shop is a design exercise, not a real shop, and nothing on it is for sale. If you join its waitlist it stores your email address, which option you picked and any note you left, so I can send you one message if the idea ever becomes real. The pricing page's quote form stores the contact details and project notes you enter so I can reply with a quote. Both also store a hashed IP and user agent, and both notify me over Telegram.

Legal basis: your consent, given by submitting the form. Kept: waitlist entries until the idea is abandoned or you ask to be removed, quote requests for 12 months.

Things you save in the signed-in tools

The workout log, the flight tracker, the plant and recipe tools and the vocabulary trainer save what you put into them against your account. The flight tracker in particular holds GPS traces, which are location data about you. Nobody else can read any of it, and it is included in the export and the deletion described below.

Legal basis: performance of a contract. Kept: until you delete the item or the account.

Files you upload

Photos and audio you upload are stored on the server. Photos have their EXIF metadata stripped on upload, so the camera details and any GPS coordinates baked into the file are discarded before it is saved.

Bouquets you share

Sharing a bouquet from the flowers page writes the bouquet and your note to a file with a random link. Anyone holding the link can read it, which is the point, so do not put anything private in the note. These are deleted automatically after seven days.

Server logs

The web server keeps ordinary access logs containing IP addresses, timestamps and requested URLs, as essentially every web server does. They are used for debugging and for spotting attacks. Legal basis: legitimate interest in keeping the site running.

Analytics, only if you agree

If you accept analytics, Google Analytics records which pages you visit and roughly where in the world you are. It does not run at all unless you say yes, and you can change your mind at any time in the box below. Legal basis: your consent.

Local storage is not the same thing

Most of the games and tools keep their state in your browser's local storage: the puzzle you are halfway through, a draft, which tab you had open. That never leaves your device and I cannot see it. Clearing your browser data clears it. The one exception is the vocabulary trainer, which merges your local practice history into your account when you sign in, so it survives a new device.

Who else sees your data

I do not sell anything to anyone, and there is no advertising on this site. These are the only third parties involved:

  • Hetzner, in Germany, hosts the site and its database. Everything described above physically sits on their servers, inside the EU.
  • Telegram delivers the alert that tells me a contact message or waitlist signup has arrived. The content of that alert includes what you typed into the form, so Telegram handles it in transit.
  • Google verifies your identity when you sign in with a Google account, and runs Analytics if you have accepted it.
  • Tawk.to runs the chat widget on the home, about and thesis pages, if you have accepted chat. What you type into the chat goes to them.

Content delivery networks

Some pages load code libraries from public CDNs (Cloudflare's cdnjs, jsDelivr, unpkg and the Tailwind CDN). Loading a file from those exposes your IP address to them, as loading any file from any server does. Webfonts used to work this way too and no longer do: every font on this site is served from this domain, so your browser never contacts Google to draw the text.

The IP Atlas page

One page on this site is a tool for looking up IP addresses. When you press the button to look up your own, your browser queries a set of public geolocation services directly, so those services see your address. Nothing is sent until you press it and nothing is stored here. The map tiles on that page come from CARTO.

Transfers outside the EU

Google and Tawk.to are United States companies and Telegram operates outside the EU. Where data reaches them it leaves the EEA. Google is certified under the EU to US Data Privacy Framework. The amount of data involved is small and, for analytics and chat, only happens if you agreed to it.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask me to:

  • tell you what I hold about you, and give you a copy of it,
  • correct anything that is wrong,
  • delete it,
  • hand it over in a machine readable file, or send it somewhere else,
  • stop or limit a particular use of it,
  • and withdraw a consent you gave earlier, which is as easy as giving it.

For an account, the first, fourth and third of those are buttons rather than letters: the account page will export everything held against your account as a JSON file, and will delete the account and its contents for good. Deletion there is real deletion, not a hidden flag.

For anything else, write to contact@domenhribernik.com and I will deal with it within a month. There is no charge.

If you think I have handled your data badly, you can complain to the Slovenian supervisory authority, the Information Commissioner (Informacijski pooblaščenec), www.ip-rs.si. You are welcome to raise it with me first, but you do not have to.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes in a way that affects what you agreed to, the version number at the top goes up and the cookie question is asked again. Small corrections happen without ceremony.
